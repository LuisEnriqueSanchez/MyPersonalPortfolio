### 1. Executive Summary

My role within the RTULe Formula Student team focused on **mechanical surfacing and integration design**. The project's core challenge was to ensure **maximum curvature continuity** in the *outer shell* for aerodynamic performance, and to optimise the design for the intended **carbon fibre lay-up and manufacturing process** while integrating complex components like the air intake duct.

---

### 2. Problem Definition and Technical Requirements

The design process was governed by critical technical and physical constraints typical of high-performance racing vehicles.

* **Aerodynamic Requirement (Outer Shell):** The exterior surfaces demanded high curvature continuity ($\text{G}2$) to maintain attached airflow, thereby minimising parasitic drag and maximizing the efficiency of the aerodynamic devices.
* **Integration Constraint (Air Intake):** The air intake duct design required intricate CAD work to fit precisely into a severely **limited space** within the engine's *packaging*. The design also needed to ensure the internal geometry preserved a stable **laminar flow** path towards the engine.

---

### 3. Process, Design, and Methodology

My approach involved rigorous mechanical surfacing techniques and an acute awareness of manufacturing limitations.

* **Advanced CAD Modelling (Inventor):** I utilised **Inventor** to perform complex free-form surfacing. The focus was on the **precise control of guide curves and surface patches** to achieve the required $\text{G}2$ geometric quality, which is critical for smooth airflow.
* **Design for Manufacture (DFM) & Composites:** The design was explicitly optimised for the subsequent **carbon fibre lay-up process**. This involved setting specific design features (e.g., radii, flange design) to facilitate efficient fibre stacking, minimize wrinkles, and ensure successful component demoulding and assembly.
* **Component Integration:** A highly precise transition geometry was generated for the air intake duct, successfully solving the challenging *packaging* issue while preserving the critical flow characteristics vital for engine performance.

---

### 4. Technical Results and Conclusions

The project successfully delivered CAD models ready for tooling manufacture, meeting both the strict aerodynamic demands and the manufacturing feasibility requirements.

* **Validation:** The final CAD model was validated against the packaging envelope and aesthetic requirements of the team.
* **Key Competency:** This work validated my expertise in **high-quality CAD surfacing (Inventor)** and my practical understanding of **Design for Manufacture (DFM) constraints** when working with advanced composite materials.
