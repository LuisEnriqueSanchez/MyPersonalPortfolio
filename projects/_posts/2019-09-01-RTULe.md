### 1. Executive Summary

During my tenure with the RTULe Formula Student team, I contributed to the **mechanical surfacing and integration design** of the vehicle's outer shell and air intake duct.  
The main challenge was to maintain **curvature continuity** in the outer shell for aerodynamic efficiency while ensuring that the design could be **manufactured with carbon fibre laminates**.  
This project offered a first-hand experience in balancing **aesthetic, aerodynamic, and manufacturability requirements** in a competitive engineering environment.

---

### 2. Problem Definition and Technical Requirements

Design decisions were driven by both **aerodynamic performance goals** and **physical packaging constraints**:

* **Aerodynamic Requirement (Outer Shell):** Achieve smooth, continuous surfaces ($\text{G}2$) to reduce parasitic drag and maximise airflow efficiency over the car's body.  
* **Integration Constraint (Air Intake Duct):** Adapt the air intake geometry to fit precisely into the limited engine packaging, while preserving **laminar airflow** towards the engine.  

---

### 3. Process, Design, and Methodology

* **Advanced CAD Modelling (Inventor):** Executed **complex free-form surfacing**, focusing on guide curves and patches to reach the necessary geometric quality.  
* **Design for Manufacture (DFM):** Optimised features to facilitate **carbon fibre lay-up**, including radii, flanges, and smooth transitions to prevent fibre wrinkling.  
* **Component Integration:** Developed precise transitions for the air intake duct, addressing the **space constraints** while maintaining airflow performance.

### 4. Technical Results and Conclusions

* Delivered **CAD models ready for tooling manufacture**, meeting aerodynamic and manufacturing constraints.

* Validated the design against packaging envelopes and aerodynamic requirements, confirming a **functional and manufacturable solution**.
